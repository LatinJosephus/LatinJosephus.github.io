The Latin Josephus Project

Flavius Josephus

Flavius Josephus (c. 37–100 CE) was a historian who wrote the Antiquities, a history of the Jews up to Roman times, and the Jewish War, describing the Jewish rebellion against the Romans in 66–73 CE, as well as the Against Apion, a defense of Jewish traditions.

While Josephus originally wrote these works in Greek, they were extremely popular and influential during late antiquity and the Middle Ages in Latin translations, as well as in reimaginings, like the De excidio Hierosolymitano. James J. O’Donnell suggested that the Antiquities was “the single most often copied historical work of the Middle Ages”. Our new list of Latin Josephus manuscrits (over 500) speaks for itself in this regard.

Before the completion of our text in 2019, the most recent publicly available edition of the Latin Antiquities was that of J. Froben, printed in 1524.

This Project

This site now offers a transcription/edition of all the Latin versions of Flavius Josephus’ works:

  • The Jewish Antiquities, in 20 books, translated s. VI under Cassiodorus. Complete.
  • The Jewish War, namely the literal Latin translation in 7 books, translated c. 400. Complete.
  • The Against Apion in 2 books, translated at the same time as the Antiquities. Complete.
  • The De excidio Hierosolymitano or DEH (On the Ruin of Jerusalem), a heavily reworked version of the Jewish War in five books. Attributed to Josephus until at least 825, thereafter to “Hegesippus” (aka Ps. Hegesippus). Complete.

Translations are provided for each of these texts.

This project also now reproduces nearly every early medieval manuscript annotation to these texts.

Project History

At UBC, in 2013, we began with the Latin Antiquities, based on a Carolingian era (s. IXmed) copy: Bamberg Msc.Class.78 (Blatt, no. 113). The Latin was aligned with the Greek text (from Niese), which was then aligned with the English translation produced by Whiston in 1737.

From 2016 to 2019, at UQAM (with generous support of the FRQSC), a great deal of progress was made. Books 9 to 20 of the Antiquities were transcribed by Maxime Laprade, Jonathan di Gregorio and Jean-Félix Aubé-Pronce. Also, in June 2017, Richard Pollard produced an electronic text of the complete Latin Jewish War, using the edition of Edward Cardwell from 1837, which is indirectly based on the Galenius edition of 1534.

The complete Latin text, transcribed over a number of years, was made available on 28 May 2019. It was the first complete new edition of the Latin Antiquities since 1524.

In 2021, the first 8 books of the Antiquities were carefully corrected and manuscript annotations were transcribed.

In spring 2022, the project moved to a website built by Performant Software Solutions, with improved functionality (LatinJosephus v. 2.0). Early medieval annotations began to be integrated into the online edition.

In autumn of 2026, the content and website were thoroughly updated and expanded (LatinJosephus v. 3.0):

  1. The DEH was added, first in Ussani’s Latin, then with English, then French, then Italian translations. All of its early medieval manuscript annotations were added as well.
  2. The Against Apion was added.
  3. The previous Latin texts of the Antiquities and Jewish War were carefully corrected, removing thousands of mistakes.
  4. Greek text was added for the Jewish War.
  5. The Antiquities, Jewish War, and Against Apion were provided with Niese section segmentation, as well as various other divisions (e.g. Bamberg manuscript segmentation).
  6. An English translation by Thomas Lodge (1602), based on the Latin, was added for the Antiquities, the Jewish War, and the Against Apion.
  7. A powerful search and concordance tool was added, covering all original language texts, translation, and even annotations.
  8. The most comprehensive list to date of Latin Josephus manuscripts was added.

For details about the editions, translations and textual correspondences used in the readers, see Texts.

Corrections

The Latin transcription was very thoroughly checked and corrected in autumn of 2026. Few errors should remain. If you spot any, please contact pollard.richard_matthew@uqam.ca.

How to cite:

Give the work, the passage reference displayed in the reader, a link to the passage, and the date of access. For the Antiquities, Bellum Judaicum and Contra Apionem, use the book and Niese section where available. For De excidio Hierosolymitano, use the book, chapter and paragraph, or the Prologue paragraph. Suggested forms for the Latin texts are:

  • Flavius Josephus (Latin trans.), Antiquities, book and Niese section, eds. R.M. Pollard, J. Timmermann, J. di Gregorio, M. Laprade, and J.-F. Aubé-Pronce, 2013–2019, revised 2026, Latin Josephus Project.
  • Flavius Josephus (Latin trans.), Bellum Judaicum, book and Niese section, ed. E. Cardwell (1837), electronic edition by R.M. Pollard, 2017, revised 2026, Latin Josephus Project.
  • Flavius Josephus (Latin trans.), Contra Apionem, book and Niese section, ed. K. Boysen (1898), electronic edition by R.M. Pollard, 2026, Latin Josephus Project.
  • De excidio Hierosolymitano, book, chapter and paragraph, ed. V. Ussani (1932), electronic edition by R.M. Pollard, 2026, Latin Josephus Project.

When citing a Greek text, a translation, or a manuscript annotation, also identify the editor, translator or manuscript shown in the reader.

Some Other Resources Relating to the Latin Josephus

Latin Editions of Josephus

  • Franz Blatt, ed., The Latin Josephus (Aarhus, 1958): incomplete, only Antiquities I-V.
  • Johannes Frobenius, ed., Flavii Iosephi Opera (Basel, 1524): the last Latin edition that was not ‘improved’ by comparison with the Greek text.
  • Edward Cardwell, De Bello Judaico (Oxford, 1837): the basis for the complete Latin text of the Bellum presented here.
  • Karl Boysen, ed., Flavii Iosephi opera ex versione Latina antiqua. Pars VI: De Iudaeorum vetustate sive Contra Apionem libri II (CSEL 37; Vienna, Prague and Leipzig, 1898): the basis for the Latin Contra Apionem presented here.
  • Vincenzo Ussani, ed., Hegesippi qui dicitur Historiae libri V. Pars prior: Textum criticum continens (CSEL 66.1; Vienna and Leipzig, 1932): the basis for the Latin De excidio Hierosolymitano presented here.
  • Bernd Bader, ed., Josephus Latinus: De Bello Iudaico. Buch 1 (Palingenesia 119; Stuttgart, 2019): a critical edition and commentary on Book I of the Latin Jewish War.
  • Randolf Lukas, ed., Josephus Latinus, Antiquitates Judaicae. Buch 6 und 7. Einleitung, Edition und Kommentar zur Übersetzungstechnik (Bochumer Altertumswissenschaftliches Colloquium 112; Trier, 2022): a critical edition of Books VI–VII of the Latin Antiquities.

Greek Editions of Josephus

  • Benedict Niese, ed., Flavii Iosephi opera (Berlin), vol. 1 (1887, Ant. I-V); vol. 2 (1885, Ant. VI-X); vol. 3 (1892, Ant. XI-XV); vol. 4 (1890, Ant. XVI-XX & Vita); vol. 5 (1889, Contra Apionem); vol. 6 (1894, Bellum Judaicum): the best critical edition of the Greek original.
  • Arnoldus Arlenius, ed., Flavii Josephi opera (Basel, 1544): editio princeps of the Greek text of Josephus.
