Millet 1556 — French

Jean Millet, of Saint-Amour, translated the Latin De excidio Hierosolymitano, historically attributed to Hegesippus, into French. His Les cinq livres de l’histoire d’Egesippe, contenans plusieurs guerres des Iuifs, & la ruine de Hierusalem was published at Paris by Guillaume Cavellat in 1556. The colophon names the printer Benoist Prévost and 17 January 1556.

Read Millet alongside DEH.

The modern Ussani Latin text (1932) provides the correspondence framework. It is not claimed to be Millet’s sixteenth-century exemplar. Millet’s books, printed chapters, pagination, dedications and wording remain independent of Ussani’s divisions. A passage correspondence does not establish word-for-word equivalence or a Latin textual variant.

Transcription conventions and editorial status

Historical spelling, grammar, names, punctuation and occurrence-specific oe/œ remain unchanged. Long s is normalized to ordinary s; genuine nasal abbreviations are expanded. Every continued word carries ¬, whether or not a hyphen was printed. The reading display flows across physical printed lines and joins words divided by ¬. These markers and the exact original whitespace remain unchanged in the source XML, frozen site data and source-coordinate spans. Display-only separators and hidden division markers preserve an exact map from every source interval to the highlighted text. Native paragraph and heading groups remain distinct; Unicode is never normalized.

The sequence a͑ is exactly U+0061 followed by U+0351 COMBINING LEFT HALF RING ABOVE, described as a with curved distinguishing mark. Its contemporary name and function remain under investigation. Ordinary a, à and nasal abbreviations have not been globally converted. Handwriting is kept separate from the printed running text.

This is a provisional scholarly transcription and passage alignment. Four pilot attention flags, including I.8.1, 24 ancillary questions, the historically pending B001-B009 and two source-layer notices remain unresolved. Annotation cards record additions, contractions, displaced material and translation differences without rewriting the reading text. They do not duplicate its primary coverage.
